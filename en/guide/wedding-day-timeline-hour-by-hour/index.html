Wedding day timeline hour by hour

A typical wedding reception lasts about 6 to 8 hours: the welcome, dinner with toasts, the first dance, dance sets, a high point with the cake and a calm closing moment. The best programmes keep active parts to 20–30 minutes and give guests time to eat and talk in between.

Wedding reception timeline: an hour-by-hour example

Here is a sample timeline for a reception that starts at 5 pm. Adjust it to your ceremony, travel time and photo session.

What matters most is the order and the rhythm: an active part, then calm time at the table.

  • 5:00 pm – welcome of the newlyweds, drinks, greetings.
  • 5:45 pm – guests seated, host introduction, first toast.
  • 6:30 pm – main course, short speeches from parents and best man.
  • 7:30 pm – first dance and the first dance set.
  • 8:30 pm – an interactive part that brings both families together.
  • 9:30 pm – second hot course, free time.
  • 10:30 pm – high point: cake, surprise, a song for the couple.
  • 11 pm–1 am – dancing, traditions, a calm closing ritual.

When to cut the cake and when to have the first dance

The first dance usually works best after the first main course. Guests have eaten, the light is still good for photos and the dance opens the floor.

Cut the cake at the high point of the night, often between 10 and 11 pm. All guests are still there, including older relatives who leave earlier.

From experience: at this point I pick up my acoustic guitar and sing a song for the couple. It is short and personal, and guests remember it.

How to avoid boring gaps without overloading the programme

Boring gaps happen when nobody knows what comes next, or when the programme is so packed that guests get tired. In both cases the fix is good pacing.

You do not need to fill every minute. A pause with good music and food is part of the night. Guests came to see you and each other.

  • Active parts up to 20–30 minutes, then time at the table.
  • Three or four bigger programme blocks per night.
  • Speeches grouped in one or two places.
  • Meal times agreed with the venue in advance.
  • Games optional, with no pressure on guests.

What the host does on the day and how they work with the photographer and DJ

The host arrives early, checks the room, sound and microphones, and confirms timings with the venue manager. During the reception they run the programme, control the pace and connect guests of different ages and languages.

Much of the work happens backstage. Before every key moment the host gives the photographer and DJ a heads-up, so nobody misses the first dance or the cake.

What if the sound system fails? From experience: I once had the sound equipment break an hour before an event, during the sound check. That is why the check happens early. The team found a solution and the event went ahead as planned.

The host questionnaire and seating plan

Fill in the host questionnaire about a month before the wedding. Include what the host cannot learn any other way.

When planning seating, think about conversation. Seat older guests away from the speakers and younger guests near the dance floor. Guests from both families who share a language or interests can sit together.

  • How you met and key moments in your story.
  • Who gives speeches and in what order.
  • Guests' languages and ages, who gets on with whom.
  • Topics to avoid.
  • Traditions to keep and ones to skip.
  • First dance song and other music requests.

Wedding planning checklist: six months out

Planning a wedding in six months is realistic if you make the main decisions in the first month.

  • 6 months: date, budget, guest count, venue, ceremony location.
  • 5 months: host, DJ, photographer, videographer.
  • 4 months: invitations, outfits, decor.
  • 3 months: menu, cake, accommodation for guests.
  • 2 months: dance lessons, rings, transport.
  • 1 month: host questionnaire, seating plan, final guest list.
  • 1 week: timeline for every supplier, contact list.

Ending the night beautifully and the second day

End the night on purpose: a slow last dance, a circle of guests with phone lights around the couple, or a short thank-you from the newlyweds.

A second wedding day is usually relaxed: brunch or lunch, a barbecue, conversation and outdoor games for children. One or two short programme moments are enough.

Questions

How long does a wedding reception last?

Usually 6 to 8 hours from the welcome to the closing ritual. In Polish-style weddings it often runs until morning. The DJ can continue after the host's programme ends.

How to plan a wedding reception programme?

Start with fixed points: welcome, meals, first dance, cake. Place three or four short blocks between them. Leave time for conversation.

When to cut the wedding cake?

Usually at the high point of the night, around 10–11 pm. All guests are still in the room then.

When should the first dance happen?

Right after the first main course, about one and a half to two hours into the reception. It opens the dance floor for everyone.

How to avoid boring gaps at a wedding?

Share one clear timeline with the host, DJ and venue. Keep active parts short and fill transitions with music.

How not to overload a wedding programme?

Limit bigger blocks to three or four per night, each up to 20–30 minutes. Keep games optional.

What are good ideas for welcoming the newlyweds?

Bread and salt from the parents and a glass of champagne are classic. You can add live music or a guest tunnel with bubbles. Keep it short, guests have already waited.

What to include in a wedding questionnaire for the host?

Your story, speakers, guests' languages and ages, traditions, music and topics to avoid. Fill it in about a month ahead.

What does a wedding host do on the day?

Checks the room and sound, aligns timings with the venue, DJ and photographer. Runs the programme and handles surprises.

What if the sound system fails at a wedding?

That is why the sound check happens early, leaving time to react. An experienced team has a backup plan. From experience: even when equipment broke an hour before, the event went ahead.

Any wedding seating plan tips?

Seat people by conversation: language, age, interests. Older guests away from speakers, younger ones near the dance floor. Avoid seating people with tension together.

What is a good second wedding day programme?

Relaxed: brunch, barbecue, conversation, outdoor games. A couple of short moments is enough.
