How to write an event script and host it

A team that laughs together works together.

Write an event script starting from the goal and the clock: first decide what guests should leave with, then split the event into blocks with exact timing, a responsible person and a technical cue. Host lines should be short, easy to say and leave room to improvise. Plan a backup for every block, because something almost always changes.

The structure of an event script

A script is a working tool for the whole team. The host, the DJ or sound engineer, the photographer, the organiser and the performers all read it. So it must be clear to anyone, even someone who opens it for the first time minutes before the start.

Start with one sentence: why does this event exist? For example, “to thank the team and present next year's goals”. That sentence helps you decide what stays and what goes.

  • Time: start time and length in minutes.
  • Block: what happens (welcome, talk, award, break).
  • Who speaks or performs on stage.
  • Host lines or key points.
  • Tech: music, microphones, lighting, slide on screen.
  • Plan B if the block runs late or is cancelled.

Conference host script example

A conference host script has three jobs: welcome people, link the talks and keep time. Write the way you speak, in short sentences. Below is a sample sequence you can adapt to your own event.

  • Opening: “Good morning. We are glad you are here today. Over the next three hours we will hear four talks and leave time for questions.”
  • Housekeeping: “Coffee is after the second talk. You can ask questions after each session.”
  • Speaker introduction: “Our next speaker has worked on [topic] for [years]. Today they will show us [specific benefit for the audience]. Please welcome [full name].”
  • Transition: “Thank you, [name]. What we just heard leads us straight to the next question…”
  • Closing: “Thank you to our speakers and to all of you. The slides will come by email. See you next year.”

How to host an awards ceremony

Rhythm is everything at an awards ceremony. Every category follows the same order: title, short explanation, nominees, winner, handover, photo. When the order is clear, the audience relaxes and waits for the result.

Check the pronunciation of every name in advance. Agree who presents each award, where the winner stands for the photo and which music plays. If winners give speeches, ask them to keep to one minute. Peter Bravvo hosted the Miss Polka Litwy pageant, where this order was essential: every step on stage had its own time and its own music.

Compering a concert: how to introduce a performer

A concert compère works for the artists and for the audience. The job is to build anticipation and then hand over the stage. Keep the introduction short: 20–40 seconds is enough.

A good formula: who the artist is, why they are here tonight, one interesting detail and a clear call with the name at the very end. Say the name last, loud and clear, then leave the stage at once. Before the concert, check with the artist's team how they want to be introduced and when the music starts.

From practice: Peter Bravvo has shared the stage with Maryla Rodowicz, Krzysztof Cugowski, Edyta Geppert, Enej, Zenek Martyniuk, Sasha Song and Biplan. Teams of big artists value precision: the host says the agreed lines and hands over the stage on time.

How to hold an audience's attention and manage time

Attention drops when blocks are too long and too similar. Mix formats: a talk, a video, music, a short interview with a guest, a break. After every longer block, give people a moment to move or chat.

Plan time with a buffer. Build a few free minutes into every hour. Agree a signal with speakers that their time is nearly up. If the programme runs late, know in advance which block you can shorten. Serving food, photos and breaks always take longer than they look on paper.

  • Short blocks and a clear order.
  • A change of format every 15–20 minutes.
  • A time buffer in every hour.
  • One block chosen in advance to cut if needed.

What to do when something goes wrong at an event

First, stay calm. The audience mirrors the host's mood. If the host is relaxed and makes a light joke, guests often do not even notice the problem.

From Peter Bravvo's practice: once the sound system broke during the sound check, one hour before the event. The team found a solution and the event went ahead. Another time, a christening nearly ended in a fire, yet the party carried on. And in an authentic castle in Denmark, the toilets were in a completely different wing from the hall, so the programme needed longer breaks. In every case the same things helped: a plan B, teamwork with the DJ and organisers, and a calm voice at the microphone.

Questions

How do I write an event script?

Start with the goal of the event in one sentence. Then split the programme into blocks with timing, a responsible person, host lines and tech cues. Add a plan B for each block.

Is there a conference host script example?

A good script has an opening, housekeeping, speaker introductions, transitions and a closing. Write in short sentences, the way you speak. You will find a sample sequence in this article.

How do you host an awards ceremony?

Use the same order for every category: title, nominees, winner, handover, photo. Check how names are pronounced and agree the music in advance. Keep winners' speeches to one minute.

What are the key tips for compering a concert?

Keep introductions to 20–40 seconds. Agree the lines and the music cue with the artist's team. Say the artist's name last and hand over the stage straight away.

How do you introduce a performer on stage?

Say who they are, why they are here, add one interesting detail and say their name loudly at the end. Then step off so the stage belongs to the performer.

How do you hold an audience's attention?

Mix formats: talks, videos, music, interviews. Keep blocks short and breaks regular. Speak to the audience directly, but never force anyone to take part.

How do you manage time at an event?

Build a buffer into every hour and decide in advance which block can be shortened. Agree a time signal with speakers. Give food service and photos more time than seems necessary.

What should you do when something goes wrong at an event?

Stay calm and briefly tell guests what is happening. Switch to plan B and work with the DJ and organisers. The audience mirrors the host's mood.

Does the host write the script?
